Overseas

Top-down pixel survival fishing. CSD1451, Team IDK, five people. Mechanics designer and gameplay programmer.

You skipper a small boat. A random quota and a day limit sit on the HUD. Fish to pay the quota. Manage health and supply so the hull does not starve. Hit a wreck for a supply dump. Miss the quota, or hit zero health, and the run is over.

I owned fishing, the QTE skill check, shipwrecks, the health/supply bars, and shop upgrades that rewrite those numbers. I also drew the player boat textures. Quota RNG and player movement were Mandy's. Monsters, rocks, water, and day/night sat with Ethan and Renfred. Menus and shop UI sat with Timothy.

Loop

Quota versus body. Fishing spots spawn in the world; collide one, enter a fishing state, then a QTE decides the catch. Rarity changes the timing ask, not just the payout. After a catch the spot is replaced, so you cannot camp one pixel. Supply drains over time. Above 80 supply, health regenerates. Below that, health ticks faster the emptier you are. Exactly three wrecks stay in the world; a collide adds 50 supply (capped at 100) and a new wreck spawns. Fishing pays the quota. Wrecks buy time on the health curve. Upgrades I wired into the shop have to show up on those bars.

Playtests

CSD1451 user testing, 26 surveys. Testers liked the pixel art, controls, QTE, and upgrades. Pain: 15 of 26 named unrealistic quota as the worst beat. Night was too dark. HUD icons too small. Tutorial missed. Too many objects. They wanted upgrades that actually help survival. What we took: tune day length, quota, and night; make the tutorial unavoidable; cut spawn density; give upgrades a survivability job. The bars were the right systems; they were too quiet in the corner.
