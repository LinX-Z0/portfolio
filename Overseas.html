Overseas

Top-down pixel survival fishing — CSD1451, Spring 2025, Team IDK. Mechanics designer and gameplay programmer.

You skipper a small boat on a hostile ocean. A random quota and a day limit sit on the HUD. Fish to fill the quota. Manage health and supplies so you do not starve the hull. Hit a wreck for a supply dump. Miss the quota, or drop to zero health, and the run is over. Survive as long as you can.

Five people: Lin Xin, Mandy Koh, Ethan Siow, Renfred Lee, Timothy Loh. Built in C++ on DigiPen's Alpha Engine. I owned fishing, the QTE skill check, shipwrecks, the health/supply bars, and the upgrade purchases that rewrite those numbers. I also drew the player boat textures.

Design Rationale

The loop is quota versus body. Quota and days are rolled at the start of a stretch (Mandy's generator). Fishing is how you pay it down. Spots spawn in the world, you collide into one, you enter a fishing state, then a QTE decides the catch. Rarity changes the QTE so a rare fish is a different timing ask, not the same bar with a bigger payout. After a catch the spot is replaced, so you cannot camp one pixel forever.

Health and supply are the body. Supply drains over time. At 80+ supply, health regenerates. Below that, health ticks down faster the emptier you are — empty supply is the worst drain. Shipwrecks always keep three wrecks in the world. Colliding one adds 50 supply (capped at 100) and respawns a new wreck. Fishing pays the quota. Wrecks buy you time on the health curve. Upgrades I hooked into the shop change those values so a purchase has to show up on the bars, not just in a menu.

Monsters, rocks, day/night, and water are the pressure around that loop (Ethan and Renfred). Playtests later said the quota and the night vignette were too mean, and the HUD too small. That is the same loop: if the bars and the quota are unreadable, the player cannot make the greedy-or-safe call the systems were built for.

My role

Backlog assignee on fishing, UI bars, shipwrecks, QTE, and upgrades. Authored Fishing, FishingSkill / QTE, shipwrecks, and Bar. Co-author on Game.cpp. Player movement and quota RNG were Mandy's; I added the boat textures on that player.

  • Fishing spots: spawn, collision, animated markers, catch-driven replacement, maintain a population of spots.
  • Fishing skill / QTE: fishing vs none state, rarity-init QTE, input check, green/red/white overlay bars.
  • Health and supply HUD: 11-frame sprite icons, bottom-left. Supply drains at 5/sec. Health regen above 80 supply; stepped drain at 75 / 50 / 25 / 0.
  • Shipwrecks: exactly three, random spawn, two textures, +50 supply on collide, then replace.
  • Purchasable ship upgrades, wired so they modify the same health/supply numbers the HUD reads.

Playtests

CSD1451 user testing, 26 survey responses. I observed the first session (Chen JiuLin): tutorial missed, health/supply icons too small, fishing-to-QTE delay read as “am I fishing?” The wider set agreed the pixel art, controls, QTE, and upgrades were the fun. The pain was quota: 15 of 26 named unrealistic quota as the most frustrating beat. Night was too dark. Too many objects on screen. Testers asked for more upgrades that actually help survival.

What we took: tune day length, quota, and night visibility; make the tutorial unavoidable; cut spawn density; add upgrades with a survivability job. The bars I wrote were the right systems; they were just too quiet in the corner.

Technical notes

Alpha Engine, C++. Fishing and wrecks are population rules plus collision gates. The HUD is a 1×11 spritesheet: health/10 picks the frame. Game state holds quota, allocated days, and the fail check when the day counter runs out without meeting quota. File I/O for the fish log and shop reset lived on Renfred's side of the engine. RapidJSON is in the tree for that save path.
