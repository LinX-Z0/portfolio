Overseas

2D top-down fishing survival — DigiPen Year 1, Trimester 2. Five-person team. Mechanics designer and gameplay programmer.

Overseas puts a small boat on a hostile ocean and asks you to stay alive by fishing, scavenging, and spending what you pull in on upgrades. I owned the systems that make that loop play: fishing spots, catch QTEs, the health and supply HUD, and shipwrecks as a world resource.

Design Rationale

Survival here is not a timer on a quota board. It is a body: supplies drain passively, health recovers or drops based on how full that bar is, and upgrades rewrite those numbers. If the HUD lies, the player cannot make a greedy or a safe decision. So the design problem and the programming problem were the same: every catch, miss, wreck, and upgrade has to show up as a readable change in supplies and health.

Fishing is the active verb. Spots spawn in the world, you collide with them, you enter a fishing state, then a QTE decides the catch. Rarity changes the QTE UI so a rare fish is a different ask, not just a bigger number after the same prompt. Catching a fish replaces the spot instead of leaving an infinite tap. That keeps the ocean moving: you route between remaining spots instead of camping one pixel.

Shipwrecks are the other sink and source. They spawn at random, collide, pay out supplies, then despawn and respawn so the map always holds a fixed number of wrecks. They exist so fishing is not the only way to refill, and so the water has objects you can choose to chase when a spot is empty or too risky.

My role

Mechanics designer and gameplay programmer on a five-person C++ team. Primary work was fishing, environment interactions, resources, upgrades, and HUD.

  • Fishing framework: spot spawning and collision, catch-driven spot replacement, fishing states, QTE input, rarity-aware QTE interface.
  • Health and supply loop plus HUD: passive supply drain, health regen or penalties from supply level, values modified by upgrades, sprite-based UI that updates with those states.
  • Shipwreck resources: random spawn, collision, supply rewards, removal and respawn, a fixed count of wrecks in the world.

Technical notes

Built in C++ for a Year 1 DigiPen game (Alpha Engine). The hard part is not drawing the boat. It is keeping world objects, player state, and HUD on one clock. A fishing spot that does not despawn after a catch, a QTE that does not match rarity, or a supply upgrade that does not change the drain rate all break the loop in the same way: the simulation and the picture disagree.

Spot replacement and wreck count are population rules. The ocean always has something to chase, never an infinite node. Collision is the gate into fishing and scavenging, so those volumes have to match what the player sees. The HUD is sprite-based on purpose: Year 1 UI that still has to show drain, health, and upgrade-modified values without a full widget toolkit.

Key Features

  • Fishing spots with collision, states, QTE catch, rarity UI, and replacement
  • Supplies and health as one loop, with upgrades rewriting the numbers
  • Shipwrecks as a maintained world resource

Tools & Technologies

  • DigiPen Alpha Engine
  • Sprite-based HUD
