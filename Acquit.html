Acquit

Physical courtroom strategy card game. Team of six. Design Lead. One player: investigation, one motion, then a jury race to 12.

You play defence on the O.J. Simpson trial. Build a file, lock a theory of the case, then spend that preparation on arguments. The brief asked for an adventure; the legal process is the journey. The other side is the trial deck, not a second player.

I was Design Lead. I designed the table systems: split decks, investigation, motions, trial, scoring, and the card list. I presented the game and did the number passes after playtests. I helped on the rulebook and on Figma / card layout.

Three phases

A single shared deck was easier to set up. Wrong-phase cards kept showing up at the wrong time. I split the decks: non-trial for investigation and pre-trial, arguments and tactics for court. Setup is fussier. Each phase is readable.

Investigation is hand-building. Draw a case, then spend investigation cards to peek, search, redraw, or filter evidence versus arguments. Extras leave at the end of the phase: the skill is what you keep, not hoarding.

Pre-trial is one Motion, with a real tradeoff. Suppress makes evidence stronger, but a failed evidence-backed argument hurts more. Dismiss boosts your argument rolls, then taxes you for every argument the other side still wins. Continuance is a redraw: safer, less swingy. That pick sits on the table for the whole trial — a rules overlay, not a one-shot.

Trial flips an argument with a baseline difficulty that already leans one side. You may play evidence and/or a tactic, then roll. Evidence is not generic +1: some cards only fit specific arguments; some swing the jury harder with worse odds. Synergies reward a coherent case instead of spraying every strong card. Tactics interrupt (reroll, extra evidence, replay, cancel). An empty hand can still roll naked — dry is a state, not a lock.

Jury tokens start undecided. First to 12 wins; if nobody hits 12, a draw.

Economy and playtests

About 40 cards: 10 arguments, 15 evidence, 3 motions, 7 tactics, 5 investigation. Difficulty per argument and evidence modifiers had to survive playtest without auto-win or coin-flip. Early scores swung 5–7, then 10–2, 10–2. I iterated: leftover unused cards became a score penalty, successful cards a plus. You should feel you improved the odds. The dice are not the game. The card list is that spec.
