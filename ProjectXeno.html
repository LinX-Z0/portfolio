Project Xeno

CSD2183, Team 19. Unreal prototype: a predator that feels like it learns you, without live machine learning.

Isolation's xenomorph feels adaptive. It is not training a model. A Director AI is omniscient — pacing, menace, jobs on a blackboard. A behavior tree only has senses and runs search, chase, and attack. The hunt feels personal because the director manages fear and the creature is allowed to miss.

Team 19 researched that split and presented it together. I specified how our agent would adapt, designed most of the NPC test profiles, and built most of the Unreal prototype in C++. Blueprints were team. The playable is a test bed, not a full game.

Metric gating

I owned the adaptation spec. Track how the player actually behaves. When a meter crosses a threshold, a new behavior becomes legal. Escalation is authored and readable: you can tune when the alien starts checking lockers. It does not invent an invisible cheat.

Meters we used: flamethrower uses, locker and vent hides, noise, time in line of sight, repeated escape routes. Pipeline: action → metric → threshold → gate → new behavior.

Architecture and NPCs

The team set four layers, each managing the one below: omniscient pacing, high-level strategy, mid-level execution, low-level search / chase / attack. I implemented toward that structure in the prototype.

NPCs were the experiment around the predator. I designed most of three reaction profiles: Civilian (long delay, freezes), Technician (a beat to assess, then acts), Ghost (zero delay, tracks the xenomorph, stays out of the danger zone).
