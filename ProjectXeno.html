Project Xeno

CSD2183, Team 19 — studying the perfect predator

Alien: Isolation is a first-person survival horror set fifteen years after Alien (1979). You are trapped on a station with a xenomorph that seems to learn you. The reputation is "an AI predator that adapts." The research question for this study was what that system actually is, and how we would rebuild the feeling without making an unfair learning machine.

Design Rationale

The xenomorph does not do machine learning mid-playthrough. Creative Assembly's trick is a split brain. A Director AI is omniscient: it sets pacing and a menace gauge, posts jobs onto a blackboard. A behavior tree is not omniscient: it only has senses, polls those jobs, and executes search, chase, and attack on its own. The player feels hunted by something that "figured them out." What they are feeling is a director managing fear, plus a creature that is allowed to be fallible.

What we wanted to avoid: an unstoppable learner that outpaces the player's ability to adapt. Escalation should invite new tactics, not punish you with an invisible advantage you cannot read. What we wanted to build: an agent that tracks player-behavior metrics and unlocks gated behaviors over time, tailored to how you personally play — the same shape as Director + tree, not a neural net.

Not a self-learning machine. A systemic illusion of one.

Technical architecture

Decision-making is layered. Each layer manages the one below it:

  • Omniscient — pacing curves so escalation does not become frustration
  • High-level — processes senses, augments or swaps strategies
  • Mid-level — carries out the current strategy
  • Low-level — search, chase, attack

The technical core is metric gating. Example metrics: flamethrower uses, locker/vent hides, noise events, time in line of sight, repeated escape routes. Pipeline: player action ? metric tracked ? threshold crossed ? gate opens ? new behavior becomes available. That is designer-authored adaptation. You can tune when the alien starts checking lockers without it inventing a new, unreadable cheat.

NPCs are part of the experiment design, not extras. Three profiles, mostly as reaction delay and awareness:

  • Civilian — unprepared, severe delay before reacting
  • Technician — a cautious second to assess, then acts
  • Ghost — zero delay, tracks the xenomorph, constantly repositions out of the danger zone

The station should feel populated with people who fail at fear in different ways, while the predator's "learning" stays a gated, paced system. References in the deck include the Game Developer breakdown of Isolation's AI, the Noclip design documentary, GDC Vault on building fear, and Tommy Thompson / AI and Games.

Focus

  • Director + behavior tree, not live machine learning
  • Metric gating as the adaptation spec
  • Pacing that stays scary without becoming unfair
