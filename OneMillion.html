ONE million

Campaign document. Five phases, a gold curve that lands on the title, and an ending side quests can actually change.

Captain Jacob Ravens has to bring Fountain of Youth water home before his mother dies. I designed the campaign as tables: events, intensity, and gold. Phases start with a hook spike or a recovery beat, then climb. Side content is optional intensity, not padding. His ship as a house map is The Black Diamond.

Design intent

The campaign is guided freedom. Phase 3 is a hub with a pile of side quests and exactly two loud goals: earn a ship, and learn where the fountain is. The player can wander. They still know what the wander is for. Off-route ocean bosses sit on water that is not the main track, as a warning if you are not ready and a reward if you are.

Gold is how that freedom stays honest. It buys the next verb rather than sitting as a score. Side quests pay enough to tempt a detour without stranding the golden path. The title is not flavour: accumulated gold hits one million at the end of phase 4, when the Heart of the Sea is in hand and the fountain is the only thing left.

Five phases

Back on TrackHubTwo goals: a ship, and the fountain. Side quests are the quiet layer

Atlantis and HomecomingThe fountain, then homeThe choice, the help flag, the return

Phase 1 opens on a spike rather than a tutorial corridor: breach, then the Kraken, then the ship is gone. The capture is the handoff. Phase 2 is the recovery beat — stealth and a maze — before the hub opens the map up. Later phases assume the verbs from the earlier ones instead of introducing a new toy every room.

Gold

Each phase has its own payout. The number that matters is the running total, because that is what the player can actually spend when the next verb goes on sale.

Phase 4 paying the most, and landing the accumulated total on the title, is the curve doing narrative work. The Heart of the Sea is the expensive chapter. Homecoming pays less because the spending spree is already over; what remains is the choice.

Open water still has fishing, caves, and wrecks for extra gold. Those are the detours. They have to pay enough to be worth leaving the path and not so much that skipping the path is the optimal route.

The ending

Take the water and the new world dies. Leave it and the mother dies. Help the people who asked along the way, and the fountain lets you take it. Side quests are that flag. The event table has to track who was helped, or the true ending is a cutscene with no cause.

That is why the hub's quiet work is not optional flavour. Ghosts at a restaurant, a farmer's crops, ore for a blacksmith: each one is a line in the ending condition. Skipping them is a legal play. It is also how you lock yourself into the cruel binary.
