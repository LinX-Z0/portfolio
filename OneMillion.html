Campaign map design — Jacob's Crazy Journey

Captain Jacob Ravens has treasure, fame, a legendary ship, and a dying mother. The Fountain of Youth sits beyond the Ice Wall. He sails into an uncharted world of monsters, wizards, volcanic prisons, and Atlantis. Main objective: bring fountain water home in time. The closer he gets, the clearer it is that some legends are hidden for a reason.

Design Rationale

This is theme-led campaign work with a mechanics-led toolkit underneath. The story decides the world; programmes, event tables, intensity curves, and gold budgets decide how the world plays. I start phases with either a hook spike (Kraken right after breaching the Ice Wall, intensity 8) or a recovery beat (town after the volcano), then climb toward a climax, with side content as optional intensity, not mandatory padding.

Guided freedom is the hub philosophy in Phase 3. The island is full of side quests — ghost chef, farmer, blacksmith, fishing tournament, bounty board — but two goals stay loud: earn money for a new ship, and get information on the fountain. Off-route ocean has hard bosses. That is a warning for players who would get lost, and a reward for players who go looking anyway.

Gold is not flavour. Each phase has a resource table so the economy can actually buy the next verb (a ship at 25,000 base, for example) without stranding anyone on the golden path, while side quests still pay enough to tempt detours. Phase totals step 80k ? 250k accumulated ? 500k ? 1,000,000 ? 1,325,000. The title is the budget.

The ending is a moral machine. Take the water and the new world dies. Leave it and the mother dies. Help every person who asked for help along the way, and the fountain lets you take it. Side quests are not collectibles; they are the true-ending flag. That only works if the event table actually tracks which help is required.

Structure and systems

Five phases, each with an event table (duration, intensity, main vs side) and a gold table:

  • Phase 1 — Beyond the Ice Wall: teach ship, cannons, salvage; Kraken spike; wreck and capture into the prison goal.
  • Phase 2 — The Great Escape: stealth/escape, colour-button maze (press three room buttons to open the main door, still solve the maze), grappling hook plus a second hook for swinging, levers, volcano exit.
  • Phase 3 — Back on Track: hub town, numbered portal forest (0?1?…?9?0), wizard trial reveals Atlantis and the Heart of the Sea as the key.
  • Phase 4 — Heart of the Sea: buy a ship, optional sea bosses, gate puzzle, pyramid layered as trap ? monster ? both ? silent approach ? boss.
  • Phase 5 — Atlantis and Homecoming: fountain choice, optional true ending, return Kraken, consequences at home.

Open water also has fishing, underwater caves, and shipwrecks as extra gold. The unedited map and programme exist so circulation and scale get argued before art. Dungeon language (buttons, grapples, portal indices, pyramid layers) is specified per phase so a later implementation is not guessing the fantasy.
