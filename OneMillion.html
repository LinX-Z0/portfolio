ONE million

Campaign document. Five phases, gold that adds up to the one million in the title, and an ending that side quests actually change.

Captain Jacob Ravens has to bring Fountain of Youth water home before his mother dies. I planned the campaign in tables: events, intensity, and gold. Each phase opens with either an exciting hook or a calmer recovery section, then builds up. Side content is optional extra challenge, not padding. His ship, drawn as a house map, is The Black Diamond.

Design intent

The campaign gives freedom with direction. Phase 3 is a hub with lots of side quests and two clear main goals: earn a ship, and find out where the fountain is. The player can wander, but they always know what they are working toward. Optional bosses sit in open water away from the main route: a warning if you are not ready, and a reward if you are.

Gold keeps that freedom meaningful. It buys your next ability or tool instead of just being a score. Side quests pay enough to make a detour tempting, but not so much that the main path stops mattering. The title is not just flavour: your total gold hits one million at the end of phase 4, when the Heart of the Sea is in hand and the fountain is the only thing left.

Five phases

Back on TrackHubTwo main goals: a ship and the fountain. Side quests are optional

Phase 1 opens with action rather than a tutorial: you break through the ice wall, the Kraken attacks, and the ship is lost. Getting captured leads straight into phase 2, a calmer section (stealth and a maze) before the hub opens up the map. Later phases build on skills from earlier ones instead of adding a new mechanic in every room.

Gold

Each phase has its own payout. The number that matters is the running total, because that is what the player can actually spend when the next upgrade becomes available.

Phase 4 pays the most, and its total lands exactly on the title's one million, so the gold curve tells the story too. The Heart of the Sea is the expensive chapter. Homecoming pays less because the spending is already over; what remains is the choice.

Open water still has fishing, caves, and wrecks for extra gold. Those are the detours. They have to pay enough to be worth leaving the path and not so much that skipping the path is the optimal route.

The ending

Take the water and the new world dies. Leave it and the mother dies. Help the people who asked for help along the way, and the fountain lets you take the water without that price. Side quests decide this. The event table tracks who you helped; otherwise the true ending would come out of nowhere.

That is why the quiet side quests in the hub matter. Ghosts at a restaurant, a farmer's crops, ore for a blacksmith: each one counts toward the ending. You are allowed to skip them, but then you are stuck with one of the two harsh endings.
