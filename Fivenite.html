This is the opposite of JumpMan, which tests precision. I wanted an approachable fighting stage: drop in, pick a weapon, and keep moving. The main theme is fighting. The second theme is freedom: you choose your weapon, and you can break and build terrain so fights are not just standing and shooting. The Fortnite joke in the title sums up the goal: carefree on the surface, but carefully designed underneath.

Design intent

Easy is a design goal, not an excuse to skip planning the difficulty. Checkpoints, heals, and ammo are placed so one death does not cost the whole stage, and so combat stays about fighting rather than managing resources. Breaking and building terrain gives players a second tool if they get bored of shooting, and lets later rooms ask for both at once.

The starting weapon is the big choice. Rooms are designed to work with whatever you picked, not one fixed weapon. That is the hard part of an easy combat stage: if every room assumes one gun, the freedom is fake. For every room to work with several weapons, my move codes had to treat "attack" as one general action rather than a specific weapon.

Move codes

Same method as the other Mega Man Maker stages: each move gets a letter, and a prime (') marks a variant. The letters are different here because the theme is combat.

CodeMove
A / A'Jump / spring jump
B / B'Jump-reflect / charged-dash reflect
C / C'Attack / break block with the weapon
E / E'Build / alternate builds to climb
FDisappearing platform

The curve

Eleven challenges, with the boss on the last page. The intensity rises like a match: an easy drop-in, combat challenges in the middle, climbing while shooting near the end, and the boss as the peak.

ZoneChallengeCodesIntensity
1Choose weapon, drop inA1
2Both reflectsB+B'3
4Combat on disappearing platformA+C+F4
5–6Break for a new weapon, then build with itA+C+C' → A+E3
9Climb while shootingC+E'5
11Boss: every moveA+B+B'+C+C'+E7

Most scores sit between 1 and 5 so the stage stays genuinely easy, but it still builds (3, 4, 5, 6, 7). It never spikes to 10. The design problem was “how do I keep a fighting stage readable and fair,” not “how hard can the last screen get.”

In zones 5 and 6, the loot changes how you move. Breaking a block for a new weapon only matters if that weapon immediately opens a path the old one could not. That way, the freedom changes how you play the map, not just what you pick from a menu.
