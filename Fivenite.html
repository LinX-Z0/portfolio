FIVENITE

Mega Man Maker. Easy combat stage. Pick a weapon, drop in, fight.

Intent

This is the opposite of 4KOMA's precision exam. I wanted an approachable fighting stage: drop in, choose a loadout, keep moving. Primary theme is fighting. Secondary is freedom — weapon choice, plus break and build so rooms are not stand-and-shoot.

Easy is a constraint, not a missing curve. Checkpoints, heals, and ammo are placed so a death does not wipe the stage and so combat stays a verb instead of a resource puzzle. Terrain manipulation exists so players who get bored of gunplay have a second tool, and so later rooms can ask for gun and terrain at once. The starting weapon is the fork: rooms are written to work with what you picked, not one scripted loadout.

Cadence

Same notation method as the other Maker stages, different alphabet because the theme is combat:

  • A jump / A' spring jump
  • B jump-reflect / B' charged-dash reflect
  • C attack / C' break block with the weapon
  • E build / E' alternate builds to climb
  • F disappearing platform

Eleven challenges, boss on the last page. Drop-in is A at 1. Reflects come in at B+B' (3). Disappearing-platform combat is the first real spike (A+C+F at 4). Breaking a block for a new weapon, then building a path with it, is the loot-changes-traversal beat. The boss is scored as A+B+B'+C+C'+E at 7: jump, both reflects, attack, break, and build. That is a full-kit exam for a fighting level, not a platforming one. Full table is in the PDF.

Pacing

The intensity curve is meant to feel like a match: low drop-in, mid combat toys, late climb-and-shoot, boss as the peak. Numbers sit mostly in 1–5 so easy stays honest; the climb is still there (3, 4, 5, 6, 7). I do not spike to 10. The design problem was “how do I keep a fighting stage readable and fair,” not “how hard can the last screen get.”
