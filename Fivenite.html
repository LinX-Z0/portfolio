Mega Man Maker. Easy combat stage. Pick a weapon, drop in, fight.

This is the opposite of JumpMan's precision exam. I wanted an approachable fighting stage: drop in, choose a loadout, keep moving. Primary theme is fighting. Secondary is freedom — weapon choice, plus break and build so rooms are not stand-and-shoot. The Fortnite joke in the title is the brief: carefree on the surface, still authored underneath.

Design intent

Easy is a constraint, not a missing curve. Checkpoints, heals, and ammo are placed so a death does not wipe the stage and so combat stays a verb instead of a resource puzzle. Terrain manipulation exists so players who get bored of gunplay have a second tool, and so later rooms can ask for gun and terrain at once.

The starting weapon is the fork. Rooms are written to work with what you picked, not one scripted loadout. That is the hard part of an easy combat stage: if every room assumes one gun, freedom was flavour text. If every room works with several, the cadence has to name attack as a family rather than as a specific button.

Cadence alphabet

Same notation method as the other Maker stages. Different alphabet, because the theme is combat.

CodeVerb
A / A'Jump / spring jump
B / B'Jump-reflect / charged-dash reflect
C / C'Attack / break block with the weapon
E / E'Build / alternate builds to climb
FDisappearing platform

The curve

Eleven challenges, boss on the last page. The intensity curve is meant to feel like a match: low drop-in, mid combat toys, late climb-and-shoot, boss as the peak.

ZoneAskCadenceIntensity
1Choose weapon, drop inA1
2Both reflectsB+B'3
4Combat on disappearing platformA+C+F4
5–6Break for a new weapon, then build with itA+C+C' → A+E3
9Climb while shootingC+E'5
11Boss — full kitA+B+B'+C+C'+E7

Numbers sit mostly in 1–5 so easy stays honest; the climb is still there (3, 4, 5, 6, 7). I do not spike to 10. The design problem was “how do I keep a fighting stage readable and fair,” not “how hard can the last screen get.”

Zones 5 and 6 are the loot-changes-traversal beat. Breaking a block for a new weapon only matters if that weapon immediately opens a path the old one could not. That is freedom doing work on the map rather than on a menu.
