FIVENITE

Mega Man Maker — easy combat level inspired by Fortnite

Fivenite is the opposite of a precision platforming exam. Inspired by Fortnite, I wanted a more carefree, almost "braindead" combat romp: pick a starting weapon, drop in, fight. Primary theme is fighting. Secondary theme is freedom — weapon choice, plus breaking and building so the rooms are not just stand-and-shoot.

Design Rationale

Easy difficulty is a design constraint, not a lack of structure. Checkpoints, heals, and ammo are placed on purpose so deaths do not wipe twenty rooms of progress and so combat stays loud instead of becoming a resource puzzle. Terrain manipulation (break blocks, build blocks, alternate-build to climb) is there so players who get bored of gunplay have a second verb, and so later rooms can ask for both at once.

The cadence still ramps. You jump in and pick a weapon (A, intensity 1), then learn jump-reflect and charged-dash reflect (B + B', 3), then fight while jumping and reflecting (2). Disappearing-platform combat is the first real spike (4). Breaking a block for a new weapon, then using that weapon to build a path, is the Fortnite beat: loot changes how you traverse, not just your DPS.

The back half mixes combat with traversal under pressure — fire waves plus a frog, spring jump, climbing by alternating built blocks while an enemy is still shooting, then a key-and-boss-door setup. The boss is intensity 7 and is scored as using everything: jump, both reflects, attack, break, and build. That is the "full kit" exam for a fighting level, not a platforming one.

Level systems / cadence

Notation:

  • A jump / A' spring jump
  • B negate/reflect damage by jump / B' charged dash reflect or damage
  • C attack with chosen weapon / C' break block with weapon
  • E build block / E' alternate building to climb a wall
  • F disappearing platform

Eleven challenges, boss on page 8. Intensity curve is supposed to feel like a looter-shooter match: low drop-in, mid combat toys, late build/fight mix, boss as the peak. Weapon choice at the start is the one systemic fork — later rooms are written to work with "the weapon you picked," not a single scripted loadout.

Skill Theme

  • Primary: fighting
  • Secondary: freedom (weapon choice, break/build)
