Death's Refrain

2D top-down grid tactics — Team Infernumb, Year 2. Design Lead. Built on Hellmaker.

Turn-based combat on a grid. Spend action points to move, strike, or guard, then enemies answer. Clear the room, take a heal or a permanent boost, and go through the portal.

Nine-person team. I designed every shipped mechanic, wrote the Lua, and integrated maps, assets, UI, SFX, VFX, and BGM so the game actually ran. I set the level flow. Some tile maps and the boss-room layout had help. Engine debugger and editor: Hellmaker.

Combat

Each player activation has 8 AP. Movement is once per turn, 1 AP per tile: highlight a path, click again to commit. Melee costs 1 AP; ranged costs 2 AP; weapon swap makes range a budget choice, not a skin. Attack mode draws line of sight — green is a legal hit, red is blocked. A shield costs 1 AP: it eats one hit, drops if you move, and cannot be used two turns in a row. You can end the activation early. Standing still is a real option.

Turns alternate. Damage is Strength minus Defense, minimum 1. The floor is part of the kit: spikes, fire that ticks each round, slow tiles that cost 2 AP to enter. Souls drop from kills. Heal, ATK, and AP orbs sit on the grid. Some rooms can also clear by securing a node or winning zone control instead of wiping every enemy.

Enemies and rooms

Scorpions chase and melee when adjacent. Tomes stay put and shoot on straight line of sight. Slimes collapse, skip a turn, then return; they do not block the portal, so they are optional pressure. The Lich in Layer 1 Room 5 is a two-phase fight (beam, then smash) sharing the room with scorpions and a tome. I designed that encounter; layout on the boss room had help.

I set the flow: tutorial, five Layer 1 rooms (boss on room 5), two Layer 2 rooms. After a clear: dialogue, a full-heal or permanent Strength / Defense choice, then the portal. I wrote those dialogue, reward, and portal beats. Enemy counts, traps, and pickups escalate so later rooms assume the verbs are already learned. Some tile maps I laid; some I integrated after help.

The tutorial always runs first. One verb at a time: move, attack and line of sight, weapon swap, what actually clears a room, shield, then souls and special tiles. Later rooms combine those lessons. Condemn / spare endings were a team idea and were cut for time. What shipped is the combat loop, not a branching true ending.

Lua and integration

I wrote the Lua state machines: player combat, enemies, HUD, pause, dialogue, pickups, rewards, and the portal. I hooked YAML scenes and tile maps into Hellmaker, connected UI to that flow, and wired audio and effects so attacks, rooms, and menus played. Art and music were produced by others; getting them into the build was mine.

I authored the GDD and gameplay wiki, presented gameplay at reviews, and did most of the playtest notes and number passes after testers broke rooms.
