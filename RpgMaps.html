These are theme-led maps. The story of the occupant comes first; the rooms exist to run that job. If you only look at the plan, you should still know who lives there and what they do all day. Tileset purchased. Layout, programme, and circulation are mine.

The campaign those maps sit beside is ONE million. The Black Diamond is the same captain's hull, drawn as a house rather than as a phase table.

What the four share

Each map has one spine. Side rooms hang off it. The test is whether a stranger can name the spine from the plan alone.

Tony and Bruce

Two jobs, two wings.

Tony extracts DNA and rebuilds dinosaurs. He cannot cook, clean, or run a household. Bruce keeps both of them alive. The house keeps those jobs apart, which is the whole programme: a mad-scientist premise only works if the assistant's labour is a circulation problem and not a joke in the corner.

Circulation

Tony's wing: lab, incubator, analytics, hatchery, nicer bath. Bruce's wing: kitchen (Tony is banned), garage, fossil path, feeding, cleaning. Fossils enter through Bruce. DNA happens in Tony's rooms. Dinosaurs live where Bruce can feed them without walking through the lab.

The ban on the kitchen is the readable rule. One closed door does more than a sign: Tony's work and Bruce's survival loop do not share a hallway, so the plan states the dependency without a caption.

The Black Diamond

Solo pirate, self-sustaining hull. Same captain as ONE million.

Jacob lives on the ship. There is no town. Food, weapons, planning, sleep, and a way off have to sit in one loop or he cannot leave port. That constraint is the design. A house map for a campaign captain is not flavour; it is the proof that the character can function before the phases start.

Circulation

A short deck circuit: entrance, cannons, kitchen, storage, office and treasure, bedroom. Kitchen and fishing are how he eats. Cannons are how he stays a pirate. The raft is the fail state. Navigation table and office are planning. Bedroom and toilet are the private life the hull still has to contain. Fast to walk. Complete enough to sail alone.

Gary the Goat Man

Goat workflow is the spine. Human rooms hang off it.

Gary is a goat farmer who built a goat-shaped house on a goat farm. The joke is the programme: animals move first. People fit around that path. If the living room were the centre, it would be a normal farmhouse with goats as decoration, which is the version I was trying not to draw.

Circulation

Goat highway: free roam and baby barn, milking and shearing, then butchery. Wool, milk, and meat storefronts turn that path into an economy. The kitchen wall opens into the baby barn so he can watch kids while he cooks. Living room, bedroom, and car are pockets off the loop. Crops, fertilizer, and compost outside close the feed cycle.

Sunrise Call High

A school that still reads as a school, reused as an outbreak base.

Students and faculty stay on campus and split it by skill. I did not skin a bunker over the plan. Gyms, toilets, canteen, classrooms, and drop-off keep their bones and take new jobs. Point at a wing and you should know rest, food, defense, or command.

Circulation

Classrooms become living quarters. The drop-off becomes screening and quarantine. Gyms and workshops become training. Greenhouse and water filtration sit where a campus can actually grow food. Command sits in the hall. Weapons sit in isolated side rooms, so the arsenal is not the living room.

Keeping the school readable is the constraint that makes the reuse honest. A base that erases the building is just a bunker with lockers. A base that reassigns the building still teaches you where you are.
