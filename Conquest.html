Conquest

Solo hex strategy. Team of six. Design Lead. Eight turns to drop the assassin's HP to zero.

You command a prince and two knights (different traits) on a numbered hex map. Pick one of four strongholds; that pick also sets where the assassin starts. Lose the party or miss eight turns, and it is over. The eight turns are a rite of passage that will not wait. The assassin peeling land as he walks is the kingdom slipping. Painting the map is fuel. The win is the kill.

I was Design Lead. I designed the hex loop, AP, supplies, the assassin, and the combat tools. I wrote the design statement and shot the demo. Event cards, the rulebook, and print-and-play layout I helped with.

Turns and supplies

Each turn opens with an event card that changes the whole board. Then three phases — morning, evening, night — with 10 AP each, tracked on a D20. Traversing a territory costs 1 AP. You spend that AP to move and to conquer; held land is how supplies arrive.

Supplies spend between turns, three ways: heal, level knights, or unlock formations. You cannot fund all three well. Levelling and formations are how the retinue gets strong enough to kill. Healing is how you live long enough to use them.

The assassin

He is a pathing problem, not a scripted boss. Shortest path to the prince, skip small nodes, faster at night. Land he walks through becomes unconquered — your economy peels off the map because he travelled. In a fight he picks from three reactive moves based on how many of you are in the hex.

Forests shroud him: a whole phase to enter or leave, which can buy you a full AP budget. Mortars (if you hold them) are a once-per-turn map-wide attack. Watchtowers work like mortars but only for a region. Terrain taxes his clock versus yours. Formations and knight levels are the combat layer once you intercept.
